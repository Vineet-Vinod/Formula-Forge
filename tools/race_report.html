Discrete paths. One race.

Can discrete racing lines and beliefs about nearby cars produce useful racing on a laptop?

Recorded race

Cars are drawn to scale. Red outlines mark overlapping rectangular footprints. The selected car's current and target trajectories are white and orange.

Experimental model

Each car selects a lap trajectory, then follows a smooth, grip-limited transition. The planner updates separate opponent beliefs from noisy, limited-range observations and chooses a line using a short risk rollout and an offline value-iteration table. This is a reduced model, with no contact response. It does not establish production vehicle handling.

The recorded overlap count matters. A position swap with contact is not a successful overtake. The report retains failures, and clean_passes excludes pairings with recorded contact before the swap. Rendering time is excluded from native tick measurements.

Measurements

Tick timings cover two 120 Hz simulation steps per 60 Hz frame, including scheduled observations and planning. The synchronized stress case puts every car's decision in the same frame. Measurements describe these recorded runs, not a real-time guarantee under arbitrary system load. 

__SUMMARY__

What this tests

Line count changes the number of possible paths without multiplying a joint state over every car. The measured minimum RMS separation is around the full lap; many paths can share a local section. The solver reports the Bellman residual and refuses an unconverged table. Short-horizon action scoring does not solve the continuous belief-space POMDP or account for the value of future information.

Speed profiles constrain curvature, acceleration and braking. Actual engine torque, tire transients, suspension, collision response, drafting and strategic opponent intent remain outside the reduced dynamics. Independent opponent beliefs omit correlations. Constant-speed longitudinal forecasts and short lateral extrapolation can fail when nearby cars brake or merge together.
