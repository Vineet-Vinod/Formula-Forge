## Source findings

The NW-facing photo supports the asymmetric upper silhouette in the study: eight main dormer/window stacks before the broad blind roof mass, three stacks after it, one separate NE service-return dormer, and one SW rounded cupola. The current study keeps these distinct. Do not copy the other cupolas visible behind the roofline onto this rear plane.

The image also shows shallow arched relief panels across the raised blind roof mass. The current study's center mass reads as a plain rectangle, so that source-specific relief remains a finite detail gap. It can be represented as shallow inset arches without changing the mass envelope.

At the registered main stack near x=842 px, visible upper-floor centers are approximately y=582, 637, 691 and 745 px: gaps of 54–55 px. The horizontal-only fit suggests about 3.2–3.4 m at its stated camera distance, while the study's corresponding intervals are 3.8 m. This supports considering an estimated 3.3–3.5 m cadence for the source-visible upper rows. Vertical camera pitch is not calibrated, so the pixel ratio is not a floor survey and does not establish the hidden lower-story schedule.

The broad 34±4 m body cornice and 5.2±1.5 m mansard rise remain plausible architectural envelopes from the reported nine stories, visible upper floor rhythm, and separate SW return imagery. The photo registration is horizontal only and carries 35 px endpoint uncertainty. It cannot establish absolute height, base datum, hidden lower rear openings, exact NE annex treatment, roof-pool canopy extent, or dormer/cupola depth. Those remain held.
