# COTA 2,220–2,380 m width qualification

**Finding:** the private imagery supports a bounded legal-width correction around 2,220–2,340 m. It does not qualify an exact white-line survey or a protection-line relocation.

The review-only primary imagery is indexed at `build/reviews/resume-20261114/cota/width-private/near2300-source-tile-index.json`. Each station entry registers the current native centre and tangent into its Esri World Imagery tile (z19), with source URLs and SHA-256. The source file explicitly records that epoch and registration accuracy are unverified. The comparison plot is `near2300-native-edges-review.png`; it marks the current legal limits at ±8 m in cyan and lateral offsets in yellow.

Across the 2,220, 2,260, 2,300 and 2,340 m samples, the current ±8 m legal edges repeatedly fall within the red/pink curb band rather than at the asphalt edge. At 2,300 m, native registered RGB samples remain gray asphalt through approximately ±6 m; the distinct red/pink band starts around ±6.5–7.5 m and continues to roughly ±9–10 m. The 2,340 m sample shows the same pattern, with the curb starting near −7 m and +7.5 m. At 2,260 m, the visible positive side starts the colored band around +6.5–7 m. The 2,220 m tile clips much of the negative side, so that section has weaker two-sided evidence.

The source-supported interpretation is therefore an approximately 12.5–13.5 m legal road span across the four-section neighborhood, with ±0.75–1 m uncertainty for image registration, faded/ambiguous linework, and unknown imagery epoch. This is a bounded reconstruction candidate, not a surveyed dimension. The legal white line itself is not consistently resolvable in these tiles; what is clearly visible is that the current ±8 m lines cross the colored curb. The station 2,380 m sample shows a wider gray asphalt span and curb farther out, broadly consistent with the current 14.7 m width. A smooth transition from the narrower span to the existing 14.7 m width by about 2,380 m is better supported than a constant 16 m width.

Keep curb/apron and legal width as separate surfaces. Beyond the curb, the images show a mixture of pale paving, green verge and darker linear features, changing sides and position across the samples. The available crop does not establish that every dark line is a barrier or service path, nor a continuous protection line. Preserve existing shoulder/protection placement pending a better registered plan or direct source measurement; do not force it to follow the proposed white-line adjustment.
