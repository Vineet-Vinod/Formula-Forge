## Finding

The orthophoto distinctly supports a broad, enclosed lawn terrace, separate pale pool apron, hedge/edge planting, and curved stepping-stone path. The V1 split between lawn, lower rock support, and upper masonry facing follows those visible land-use breaks. The 59.15 m lawn control is a reasonable architectural estimate inside the cited broad 59.0–59.3 m plateau observations. Keeping higher existing relief instead of flattening it, and recording the 59.0–59.3 range plus the 58.3–58.4 m southern-edge observations, are appropriate limits. They are not a surveyed grade.

The actual-frame study places this garden in the correct Monaco hillside setting and shows a coherent retaining edge. Its highly regular stone pattern, opaque foliage cards, and flat lighting are diagnostic geometry only; they do not demonstrate final material, canopy, or shadow fidelity. The support proof shows the two already mapped trees rooted 0.10 m into the emitted lawn at their shifted positions. The +4.4067 m and +3.7779 m root moves are plausible source reconstructions, but neither tree base nor crown height is surveyed.

## Limits to retain

- Keep pool apron and pool distinct from lawn. Individual stepping stones are clearly visible in the orthophoto but are not emitted in V1; this remains a finite detail omission, not grounds to redraw the lawn boundary.
- Do not assert the low barred opening is a pedestrian gate. Its exact opening and curvature remain unregistered.
- The southern boundary and garden-to-cliff grade remain uncertain. Do not extend the 59.15 m lawn datum into the lower ravine or use it to imply a tall masonry wall.
- The composite includes separate Hermosa V3 and Rome architecture V2 studies. This review makes no new acceptance of the three held roof objects or of their camera appearance.

The packed face and actual-tree-foot proofs establish geometric support and encoding behavior; they do not substitute for a native rendered-material review.
